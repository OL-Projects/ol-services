What to look at

  1. 1Every line names the sheet and the exact dimension or statement it came from.
  2. 2Every line carries its class: OBSERVED (written on a sheet), DERIVED (arithmetic on printed numbers), OPEN (the sheet does not give it).
  3. 3The arithmetic is printed in the Basis column, so it can be redone by hand.
  4. 4The 16 open lines each name the document that would close them.
  5. 5Subtotals are recomputed from their parts.
  6. 6No prices, no bid: quantities, nothing else.

The limits, stated here and not in the fine print

  • This is not a bid. Not a priced estimate, not a professional opinion. OL holds no RBQ licence. No construction price is attached to a single line of the document.
  • The drawings are not permit drawings. The set comes from a PIIA (architectural integration) submission, and each of the 11 sheets carries the architect's own stamp: « Ces plans ne sont pas des plans pour permis. » It is written here because it changes what the document may claim.
  • Neither the permit nor the approval has been verified. The record is a PIIA recommendation to council: neither its adoption nor the issue of a permit has been verified. That is a question for the customer, and the document asks it.
  • 16 lines stay open. No door and window schedule, no foundation plan, no structural drawing, no mechanical, electrical or plumbing drawing, no wall or roof assembly legend and no demolition plan in these 11 sheets. None is filled with a plausible number: each names the document that would close it.
  • No site visit. Quantities follow the drawings; if the plan and the site disagree, the site wins and the affected lines are re-taken.
  • Removed for publication: the civic address and street name, the owner and the client, the architect, the permit and dossier numbers, every plan image and every product specification. The quantities are unchanged.

05 · Guarantees

Four promises, written down.

Fixed price.

The number in the reply is the number on the invoice. No hourly drift, no "while we were in there". If the scope changes, you get a new price and you decide before any work continues.

On time, or 50% off.

If the agreed delivery time slips for a reason on our side, you pay half. The clock starts when you accept the price and the information is complete; time spent waiting on a missing plan does not count against it.

Errors fixed free, always.

Quantity and arithmetic errors are corrected within 24 hours at no charge, whether or not the revision is used up — and whether the job was paid or free.

Your plans stay yours.

Nothing is sold, nothing is shared with another client, nothing is used to train a model. Deleted on request, or 24 months after the job closes.

What the guarantee does not cover

A takeoff is only as good as the plan you send. Changed dimensions, missing sheets, a scope the drawings do not show — those are new work, priced before it starts. The guarantee covers our errors, not a plan that does not match the site.

10 · FAQ

Questions, answered.

What exactly is a takeoff?

A measured list of what a job needs: quantities of material by division, with a waste allowance, boxes and trim, and — in the full version — a price against each line. It is the document a contractor prices a job from. It is not a bid and not a construction schedule.

Do I need to send a CAD file?

No. A PDF, a clear photo of the plan, a DWG, or just the measurements in an email all work. A CAD file is the fastest and the least ambiguous, but it is not required.

Who signs the estimate?

A licensed contractor. OL holds no RBQ licence and does not perform construction work. The estimate is a draft prepared for a contractor to sign and present, and the contractor's review is part of what makes it usable.

Why is it $74.99 when others charge $400 or more?

Because most of the cost in a traditional takeoff is the hours, and OL does not bill hours. The $74.99 is a founding price for the first 10 clients: those clients are references, and the standard price after them is $149. It is not a permanent discount and it is not a loss leader — if it does not clear its own cost, the price or the scope changes.

What is the difference between the $25 micro-takeoff and the full takeoff?

Scope and output. $25 is one room, one trade, 24 hours, quantities only — no prices. The full takeoff covers up to 1,000 ft², includes pricing, waste, boxes and trim, and takes 48 hours. If you need a number to give a client, you need the full takeoff.

What if the plan changes after I order?

One revision is included for changes within 7 days. After that it is a new job at the same fixed price, quoted before it starts.

What if the total is wrong?

Quantity and arithmetic errors are fixed free within 24 hours, at any time — not just during the revision window. That is the point of the gate: the total is recomputed from its parts before the document is sent, so a wrong total should not reach you at all.

What if you deliver late?

You pay half. The clock starts when you accept the price and the information is complete.

Do you visit the site?

No. Everything is done remotely from the documents you send. If a job cannot be done correctly without a site visit, we say so rather than guess.

Is my plan confidential?

Yes. It is used to produce your document and nothing else: not sold, not shared with another client, not used to train a model. Deleted on request, or 24 months after the job closes.

Do you work outside Québec?

Yes. Québec invoices carry GST 5% and QST 9.975%; elsewhere in Canada, GST only; outside Canada, no Canadian tax. Prices are the same.

Can I get an invoice with my PO number, or in my company's name?

Yes. Say so when you order and it is on the invoice.

Who is behind OL?

OL — Optimization and Logistics, a registered Québec company, with the numbers above on every invoice. The work is done by an AI agent inside OL's verification gate and reviewed before it is sent.

Is this AI-generated?

Yes, and that is stated on the page rather than hidden. What makes it usable is not who typed it: it is that the document is measured, the arithmetic is recomputed, and a licensed contractor signs it.
